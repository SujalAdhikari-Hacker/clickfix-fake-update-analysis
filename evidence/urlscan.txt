READING OF THIS RESULT
----------------------
The sandbox asked for /zap and was handed a redirect to google.com. The final
page is literally Google, which scores as benign in naive scanners and gives the
host a clean-ish reputation.

The host decides what to serve based on who is asking: sandbox, datacentre IP,
wrong geography or missing browser fingerprint -> Google. A real desktop browser
-> stage-2 payload. This is geofencing + user-agent/fingerprint cloaking, and it
is why the URL "looks fine" when anyone tries to check it for you.

This is exactly the trap in "I submitted it to VirusTotal / urlscan and it came
back clean". The scan tested the incumbent's decoy, not the payload.

Same behaviour seen on the lure side: the archive only ever captured the
highporn.net redirect, never the fake-update overlay.

WHAT THE PAYLOAD PATH TELLS US
------------------------------
/zap/ is short, generic and unrelated to any brand. Kits in this family rotate
paths ( /zap/, /next/, /r/, /v/ ) and re-point an IP or domain at a fresh
build, so path-based blocking alone ages badly. Block the resolved IP + ASN
egress instead, and alert on the behaviour (IWR|IEX) rather than the URL.
